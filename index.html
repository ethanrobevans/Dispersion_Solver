These are idealized limiting criteria, not a reliable prediction of breaking onset for focused wave packets, nonlinear shoaling, or strongly unsteady waves. McCowan's criterion is intended for shallow-water depth-limited breaking.

Theory & Reference
The solver uses the linear finite-depth dispersion relation:
ω² = gk tanh(kd)
where
ω = 2π/T     and     k = 2π/L
The phase velocity is
c = ω/k = L/T
and the group velocity is
cg = c/2 [ 1 + 2kd / sinh(2kd) ]
so that the group factor is
n = cg / c
Approximate breaking limits
Hb ≈ 0.78d (McCowan, shallow-water limit)
Hb ≈ 0.142L tanh(kd) (Miche)

These criteria describe idealized limiting wave heights; breaking onset depends on wave evolution, bathymetry, nonlinearity and unsteadiness.

Relative-depth regimes
d/L < 1/20    →    Shallow water
1/20 ≤ d/L ≤ 1/2    →    Intermediate water
d/L > 1/2    →    Deep water
Assumptions Linear wave theory assumes small-amplitude surface gravity waves in an incompressible, inviscid and irrotational fluid over constant water depth.
